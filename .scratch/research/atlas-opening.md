# What should the Atlas page open on?

- Question: `site/TODO.md` → "Restructure Atlas, opening". `/{lang}/map/` opens on the same plate as Home, so it does not answer "what is there" any differently from Home. The drawer is the only place with item detail.
- Checked: 2026-09-24. Public pages, logged out, Playwright Chromium at 1440×900 (full page clipped at 2,600px). Screenshots and page text are in the session scratchpad `…/scratchpad/comp/` (throwaway). The atlas screenshots are in `…/scratchpad/final2/`.
- Extends [rfcs/0000-ui-ux-research.md](../../rfcs/0000-ui-ux-research.md). That teardown looked at map, route and progress surfaces. This note looks only at **how a catalogue or index page differs from the home page** on the same sites.

## The problem, from our own pages

- Home at 1440 (`final2/en.light.1440.png`): hero, the count line "41 ready routes and 74 mapped competencies", the legend, the full plate in overview mode, then the Tool Calling specimen.
- Atlas at 1440 (`final2/en_map.light.1440.png`): title, "115 competencies in 12 domains. Select one to see why it matters and where to start.", the Plate | List toggle, the filter key, the legend, then **the same plate with the same tiles in the same layout**. The only differences are the filters and that tiles open a drawer.
- Atlas at 390 (`final2/en_map.light.390.png`): the same stacked plate as Home, about 3,000px of region blocks. Seven of the 12 regions contain only mapped circles (Software Engineering, Systems, Data Engineering, ML Foundations, Deep Learning, Multimodal and Specializations are "0 ready of N").
- DESIGN.md already gives each page its own question: "The Atlas answers 'what is there'; Progress answers 'where do I stand'" ([site/DESIGN.md → Progress](../../site/DESIGN.md)). Home's job is set by ADR 0001: "the plate leads Home and a real-route specimen sits directly under it" ([ADR 0001](../../docs/adr/0001-editorial-print-direction.md)). Nothing in the docs gives the Atlas a view that Home does not already have.
- The data for a richer view already exists and is only shown in the drawer: the lead (`why`), the details line (level, capability type, source count, diagnostic task count, from `detailsOf()`), prerequisites with bridge flags, and related labs and projects (`itemDetails()` in `site/src/lib/summaries.ts`). `list_fields` is only `[status, target_level]` ([curriculum/presentation.yaml](../../curriculum/presentation.yaml)), so today's List view has little to show.

## The pattern

1. **Home samples or overviews, the index is complete and itemised.** Brilliant, Boot.dev and master.dev show a few paths on home and all of them on the index. Khan's home lists subjects, while its subject page lists every course with every unit. The index page is where each item carries its facts: master.dev shows total time, OSSU shows duration, effort and prerequisites, Hugging Face gives a one-line purpose, Boot.dev shows lessons and hours.
2. **When home and index show the same set, the index adds facets and nothing else.** roadmap.sh's /roadmaps repeats the home grid and adds a faceted rail with counts. freeCodeCamp's /learn repeats the home list and adds no facets. That is our situation today, and in both cases the index adds little.
3. **The picture of the whole sits on the item page, not the index.** Khan's skill-square grid is on the course page, while the subject index is plain text. roadmap.sh's diagram is on the roadmap page, while /roadmaps is a list of names. No site opens its catalogue on the diagram.
4. **The index is text, ordered by the curriculum's structure, not alphabetically.** OSSU orders by curriculum section, Khan by grade, Brilliant by path, master.dev by kind. Only printed atlases add an alphabetical index, and they add it on top of a structured contents: key map, plates, then the index.
5. **In print, the key map and the index do different jobs from the plates.** The key tells you which plate to turn to, and the index tells you where a named thing is. Neither is a smaller copy of a plate.

## Options for the Atlas opening view

All three options keep `?item=` (the drawer), the filter key, and the Home plate's tiles linking to `/map/?item=<id>`.

### Option A: the Atlas opens on a gazetteer (the itemised index)

The List becomes the default view, rebuilt as the ruled printed catalogue that Labs, Projects and Paths already use (DESIGN.md → Information architecture, "Collection indexes"). It has one section per region in vocabulary order, headed "AI Engineering · 12 ready of 26". Each ready route is one entry: the title in Newsreader linking to the route; the lead (the first passage of `why`, via `passageOf()`); the details line (level, capability type, source count, diagnostic task count, via `detailsOf()`); "Needs" as the prerequisite line with maturity and state glyphs; the labs it is practice for; and the learner's state glyph after hydration. Mapped competencies do not get entries. They run in under the region's entries as one quiet line: "Mapped, no route yet: Programming Fundamentals, Python for AI Engineering, …". The plate stays the second view (`?view=plate`).

- Evidence, not completion: the state column is the evidence-derived glyph. Nothing is counted as opened or read.
- Truthful maturity: this is better than the plate. Ready routes are full entries with their facts. Mapped items are one run-in line, not rows that look like lessons, and the region head carries "N ready of M". The seven all-mapped regions shrink to a heading and one line each, instead of a block of circles. That is the only fair way to show them.
- Route to sources: each entry shows how many exact locators and diagnostic tasks the route has before it is opened. The drawer and route page still hold the locators themselves.
- Content-driven: every piece already exists (`passageOf()`, `detailsOf()`, relations, tracked-item lookup). The list becomes the same catalogue component as the collection indexes, which fits "one version of everything" better than keeping a separate thin `list_fields` table.
- Editorial print: it is the atlas's contents section, set like the Library and collection indexes already are.
- It fits the existing filters: a filter removes entries, and "Showing 19 of 115" becomes a count the reader can see, where on the plate a filter only dims tiles.
- Costs: the page named "Atlas" no longer opens on a map, so the identity sits on Home and one click away. On a phone the entries are still long (41 ready entries), though shorter than the stacked plate once mapped circles become run-in lines. The Search page is also described as "the index of a printed atlas". The gazetteer is ordered by region and itemised, while Search is by kind and matched by title, and the two descriptions need wording that keeps that split clear.

### Option B: the Atlas opens on a key map and one regional plate at a larger scale

The Atlas opens on a small key of all 12 regions (the RegionLocator marks, one row per region, like the endpaper key). Below it is one region drawn large: every route as an entry with its details line and lead, prerequisite lines always drawn inside the region, and mapped marks named. `?group=` picks the region. The default is the learner's next-step region when there is evidence, and otherwise the region of `site.specimen`.

- Distinct from Home: the world plate on Home, a regional plate here. This is the closest match to how a printed atlas moves from key to plate, and it reuses `RegionLocator.astro` and the plate-layout code.
- Truthful maturity: fine inside a region, but all-mapped regions become a whole page of circles when selected.
- Costs: the default region is an arbitrary choice for a new visitor, and the page stops answering "what is there" at a glance, because only one region is visible. Filters ("Ready routes only", "Your state: gap") cut across regions and fit poorly with a one-region view. The ADR also says always-drawn lines "tangled into noise". Within one region there are fewer lines, but that is still a reversal that would need a prototype. It is the most new UI of the three options.

### Option C: keep the plate on the Atlas and change Home instead

Home drops the full plate for the hero, a one-line key per region ("AI Engineering 12 ready of 26 →"), and the specimen, so the plate appears only on the Atlas.

- Keeps the Atlas as the map page with the least new code, and filters stay on the plate.
- Costs: it contradicts ADR 0001 ("the plate leads Home"), so it needs a superseding ADR. Home loses the element that carries the identity. The Atlas still has no itemised view (the drawer remains the only detail). This option removes the duplicate without answering "what is there" any better, which goes against pattern 1.

## Recommendation: Option A, the gazetteer

Why:

- It matches every catalogue seen. The index is complete and itemised, text-first, and ordered by curriculum structure (Khan subject contents, OSSU tables with a Prerequisites column, master.dev and Brilliant grouped lists, Hugging Face's one-line purposes). No site opens its catalogue on its diagram (pattern 3).
- It gives the Atlas a job Home does not have. Home shows **the shape** of the roadmap (the plate) and **one route up close** (the specimen). The Atlas **lists every route with its facts**, so a learner can compare routes and choose one without opening 41 drawers.
- It helps two principles more than the plate does. Mapped items become one honest line per region instead of rows of circles (truthful maturity), and each entry says how many exact sources and diagnostic tasks stand behind it (route to sources).
- It reuses the catalogue that the collection indexes already use, so this is a replace-in-place change and adds no new component family.

What changes:

- **Atlas:** the default view is the gazetteer (`?view=plate` for the plate), and the toggle order becomes Index | Plate. Today's thin List view is replaced in place, so there is not a third view. The drawer still opens from `?item=` over the gazetteer and highlights its entry. The filter key is unchanged and now hides entries. The intro changes to something like "41 ready routes and 74 mapped competencies in 12 domains, with what each route asks and how it is checked." (The en and vi strings need owner review.) DESIGN.md → Atlas, the "Modes" line of the plate section, and the TODO item are updated in the same change.
- **Home:** the plate stays as it is (ADR 0001 holds). One adjustment: an overview tile links to the route page when it has one, and `/map/?item=` is kept for mapped marks. That way a click from Home either lands on the route or lands on the Atlas entry in context, and a visitor never sees the same plate twice. This is optional. If it is kept as it is, the drawer opening over the gazetteer is already different from Home.
- **Search:** the page description changes from "reads like the index of a printed atlas" to an alphabetical index by kind, so that "gazetteer" names only the Atlas.

Open for the owner: whether the gazetteer should show the first source locator on each entry (more "route to sources", but longer entries), or only the source count as the drawer does now.
